## Science: Find Your Cadence

Cadence is often overlooked in regards to cycling performance. Everyone has their own best cadence, but science shows it's not random. **Your ideal cadence depends on power, efficiency, fatigue resistance, and it can be trained.**

### Here's What the Research Says

**Lower cadences (70–85 rpm)** improve efficiency, meaning you use less oxygen for the same power. But they increase muscular fatigue, especially on long rides.

**Higher cadences (90–110 rpm)** reduce muscle stress but raise your heart rate and oxygen demand—useful in breakaways or to save your legs for sprints.

**Experienced riders** naturally settle into their optimal cadence, often between **85–95 rpm on flats** and **70–85 rpm on climbs**. It's a balance between comfort and economy.

### Takeaway

Your cadence isn't fixed—**it's adaptable**. Try experimenting this week:

**On endurance rides →** aim for **85–95 rpm** for efficiency

**On climbs →** drop to **75–85 rpm** to build muscle endurance

**In intervals →** include short **high-cadence drills (120–130 rpm)** for coordination and leg speed

### The Cadence Training Protocol

**Endurance rides (Zone 2):**
- Target: 85-95 rpm
- Focus: Smooth, circular pedal stroke
- Benefit: Aerobic efficiency, cardiovascular development

**Climbing:**
- Target: 75-85 rpm
- Focus: Steady power, controlled breathing
- Benefit: Muscular endurance, torque development

**High-cadence drills:**
- 3-5 x 2 minutes at 120-130 rpm
- Low resistance, focus on smooth pedaling
- Recovery: 2-3 minutes easy spinning
- Benefit: Neuromuscular coordination, leg speed

**Tempo/Threshold efforts:**
- Target: 90-100 rpm
- Focus: Sustainable power
- Benefit: FTP development, race-specific training

### Why Sevilla's Story Matters

**Longevity:**  
Still competitive at an age when most pros have retired

**Adaptation:**  
Has evolved his training and racing style to remain relevant

**Cadence mastery:**  
Uses rhythm and efficiency to compensate for natural aging

### The Sevilla Approach

What's most impressive isn't just his longevity—**it's how he's adapted**.

Sevilla has shifted his focus to **smooth, efficient cadence**, often sitting right around **90 rpm on climbs** where others grind lower. That rhythm lets him stay relaxed deep into five-hour stages and maintain steady power on long ascents.

### In His Own Words

When asked about his approach, Sevilla said:

*"Cadence is my anchor. If I keep the rhythm, the effort feels lighter, even when the watts are high."*

### What We Can Learn from Sevilla

**1. Rhythm over grinding**  
Higher cadences preserve muscular freshness over long efforts

**2. Adaptation is possible**  
Sevilla wasn't always a high-cadence rider—he trained himself to be more efficient

**3. Efficiency beats raw power**  
As you age (or when fatigued), technique becomes more important than brute force

**4. Consistency compounds**  
Sevilla's career longevity comes from smart training, not heroic efforts

**5. Find your anchor**  
Whether it's cadence, breathing, or pacing—having a rhythm to return to helps manage effort

## The Cadence Mindset

Sevilla's a reminder: **find your rhythm, and let consistency do the rest.**

Your cadence is more than just RPM—it's your connection to the bike, your management of effort, and your sustainability over long distances.
