## Coach's Corner: Protein Before Bed Boosts Recovery

Research continues to support a simple but powerful recovery tool: **a dose of protein before sleep.**

An analysis published in *Frontiers in Nutrition* (2023) showed that **20–40g of protein consumed 30 minutes before sleep** can enhance overnight muscle protein synthesis in both young and older adults, especially when paired with evening training sessions.

### Why It Matters for Cyclists

✅ **Increases muscle repair overnight**  
Your body does most of its rebuilding while you sleep. Providing amino acids during this window maximizes adaptation.

✅ **Enhances adaptations from strength and endurance work**  
Whether you did hard intervals or a strength session, pre-sleep protein helps your body absorb the training stimulus.

✅ **Doesn't interfere with fat metabolism or sleep quality**  
Contrary to old myths, eating protein before bed won't disrupt your sleep or prevent fat burning.

### The Science

*"Pre-sleep protein ingestion is an effective strategy to augment the skeletal muscle adaptive response to exercise training."*  
— Snijders et al., *Frontiers in Nutrition*, 2023

### How to Implement

**Timing:** 30-60 minutes before bed

**Amount:** 20-40g of protein

**Best sources:**
- Greek yogurt (20g per cup)
- Cottage cheese (25g per cup)
- Protein shake with casein (slow-digesting)
- Turkey or chicken breast
- Protein bar (check labels for 20g+)

**Pro tip:** Casein protein digests slowly, providing a steady stream of amino acids throughout the night. It's ideal for pre-sleep consumption.

## This Week in Science: Nitrates & Endurance

A study from the *Journal of Applied Physiology* found that **dietary nitrates** (like those in beets and leafy greens) can increase time-to-exhaustion by up to **16%** in trained cyclists.

### How Nitrates Work

Dietary nitrates convert to nitric oxide in your body, which:
- Improves blood flow
- Enhances oxygen delivery to muscles
- Reduces the oxygen cost of exercise
- Delays fatigue during sustained efforts

### The Practical Tip

Try **beet juice 2–3 hours pre-race** for maximum effect.

**Dosing guidelines:**
- 300-500ml of beet juice
- Or 6-8 whole beets
- Or beetroot powder supplement (follow label)

**Important:** Practice this in training first! Some athletes experience GI discomfort from beet juice.

### Best Food Sources of Nitrates

- Beetroot (highest concentration)
- Arugula
- Spinach
- Celery
- Lettuce
- Radishes

## Athlete Spotlight: Kristin Armstrong – Queen of the TT

![Kristin Armstrong on her time-trial bike](/assets/images/journal/riders/kristin-armstrong.jpg)
*Photo: [Peter Trimming from Croydon, England](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Kristin_Armstrong_riding_to_Gold.jpg), [CC BY 2.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/2.0/)*

This week, we discuss **Kristin Armstrong**, the most decorated female time trialist in Olympic history. A **three-time Olympic gold medalist** (2008, 2012, 2016), Kristin's dominance in the time trial discipline came after turning pro at **age 27**—proof that success isn't bound by age or timing.

### In Her Own Words

**Q: What was your secret to staying at the top over three Olympic cycles?**

*Kristin: "Consistency. I trained smarter, not just harder. And I surrounded myself with people who believed in the process—that's what got me through the tough days."*

### What Makes Her Story Remarkable

**Late Start**  
She turned professional at 27, an age when many athletes are already retiring. This proves that it's never too late to start.

**Three Olympic Cycles**  
Winning gold in 2008, 2012, AND 2016 requires incredible consistency, adaptation, and mental fortitude. Most athletes peak once—Kristin peaked three times.

**Comeback After Retirement**  
She actually retired after 2012, then came back to win again in 2016 at age 42.

**Time Trial Mastery**  
The time trial is called "the race of truth"—it's just you, the bike, and the clock. No tactics, no teammates. Pure power, pacing, and pain tolerance.

### Lessons from Kristin Armstrong

**1. Train Smarter, Not Just Harder**  
More volume isn't always better. Quality, recovery, and precision matter more than beating yourself up daily.

**2. Consistency Over Intensity**  
Showing up every day, even when motivation is low, builds the foundation for peak performance.

**3. Surround Yourself with Believers**  
Your support system—coach, training partners, family—can make or break your progress.

**4. Age is Just a Number**  
Whether you're 27 or 42, it's never too late to chase your goals.

**5. Master Your Discipline**  
Kristin didn't try to be the best at everything. She focused on time trialing and became the absolute best in the world.

### Her Legacy

Armstrong now mentors young cyclists and continues to advocate for women in sport. Her journey is a reminder that **mastery is a long game—and worth every mile.**
