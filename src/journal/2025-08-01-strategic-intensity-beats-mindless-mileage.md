## Training Insight: Why 'Just Riding More' Doesn't Always Work

A recent study in the *Journal of Strength & Conditioning Research* found that cyclists who mixed **low-cadence, high-torque intervals** into their base training improved FTP more significantly than those who just added volume.

**The key takeaway?** Strategic intensity beats mindless mileage.

### The Low-Cadence Protocol

Try adding **2 x 10-minute low-cadence intervals (45–55 RPM)** once a week to stimulate muscular endurance.

**How to execute:**
- Warm up thoroughly (15-20 minutes)
- Find a slight incline or flat road
- Shift to a big gear
- Maintain 45-55 RPM for 10 minutes at steady power
- Recover 5-10 minutes between intervals
- Focus on smooth, powerful pedal strokes

**Why it works:**
- Recruits more muscle fibers
- Builds strength-endurance
- Improves neuromuscular coordination
- Enhances force production at the pedals

**Caution:** Don't overdo it. Low-cadence work is stressful on joints and muscles. Once per week is plenty.

## Nutrition: Don't Fear Carbs—Time Them

Fuel hard rides before and during with easily digestible carbs:
- Rice
- Bananas
- Honey
- White bread with jam
- Energy gels/drinks

Taper intake after if you're in a weight-loss phase.

**The bottom line:** Underfueling during efforts just hurts performance and slows recovery. Carbs aren't the enemy—poor timing is.

### Carb Timing Strategy

**Before rides (2-3 hours):**  
70-100g of carbs (oatmeal, toast, rice)

**During rides (>90 minutes):**  
60-90g of carbs per hour (gels, drink mix, bars)

**After rides:**  
Replenish glycogen with 1-1.2g carbs per kg bodyweight within 30-60 minutes

**Rest days:**  
Moderate carb intake, focus on protein and vegetables

## Coach's Corner: Training When Tired vs. Training When Unmotivated

**Tired?** Rest or do active recovery.  
Your body is telling you it needs adaptation time. Pushing through fatigue leads to:
- Decreased performance
- Increased injury risk
- Compromised immune function
- Mental burnout

**Unmotivated?** Give yourself **15 minutes on the bike**.  
If the legs still feel like bricks, call it. If not, you just beat the biggest obstacle: the start.

**The 15-minute rule works because:**
- It lowers the barrier to getting started
- Often, momentum takes over once you're moving
- You give yourself permission to quit (which you rarely do)
- It separates true fatigue from mental resistance

## Athlete Spotlight: Laurent Fignon – The Professor

![Laurent Fignon at the 1989 Giro d'Italia](/assets/images/journal/riders/laurent-fignon.jpg)
*Photo: [Massimo Nicolodi](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Laurent-Fignon-Giro-1989.jpg), [CC BY 2.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/2.0/)*

Laurent Fignon won the **Tour de France twice** (1983, 1984)—and lost it once by just **eight seconds**, the narrowest margin in history.

### The Man Behind the Glasses

Known as **"The Professor"** for his wire-rim glasses, Fignon wasn't just a strong rider—he was fiercely independent. He:
- Trained by feel, not just by numbers
- Challenged norms and questioned authority
- Rode with deep self-belief and intellectual curiosity

### The 1989 Tour: Eight Seconds

After the crushing 1989 Tour loss to Greg LeMond in the final time trial, Fignon didn't collapse—he **reflected, accepted, and moved forward**.

Losing by 8 seconds after 3 weeks of racing would break most riders. Fignon turned it into motivation.

### His Philosophy

*"Sometimes, when we train, we simply have to go out to meet the Man with the Hammer."*  
— Laurent Fignon

**Translation:** You have to experience failure, bonking, suffering, and defeat to truly understand your limits and grow stronger.

Fignon didn't avoid the hard moments—he embraced them as necessary teachers.

### Lessons from The Professor

**1. Train with intelligence, not just volume**  
Fignon used his brain as much as his legs. He questioned methods, experimented, and refined his approach.

**2. Accept defeat and learn from it**  
Losing by 8 seconds could have ended his career. Instead, it defined his resilience.

**3. Ride with independence**  
Don't blindly follow trends. Understand *why* you're training a certain way.

**4. Embrace suffering as a teacher**  
The Man with the Hammer will find you eventually. When he does, learn from the experience.
