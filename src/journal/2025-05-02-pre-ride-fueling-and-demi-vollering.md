## Performance Insight: Nailing Your Pre-Ride Fueling

Pre-ride nutrition is one of the most overlooked performance tools. Here's what the science—and top riders—say works best:

### The 3-4 Hour Window

**Eat a high-carb meal 3–4 hours before your ride.**  
Aim for **1–1.5g of carbs per kg of body weight**. This tops off liver glycogen and improves performance (Jeukendrup et al., 2014).

Example for a 70kg (154 lb) rider:
- 70-105g of carbohydrates
- That's roughly: 1-2 cups of oatmeal with banana, or 2-3 slices of toast with honey and jam

### The 15-30 Minute Top-Off

**Top off 15–30 minutes before the start** with 30g of quick carbs (e.g., gel or drink mix) to maintain blood glucose (Cermak & van Loon, 2013).

This keeps your blood sugar stable as you start the ride and prevents that initial "bonk" feeling.

### What to Avoid

**Avoid high fat, high fiber, and excess protein before the ride** to prevent GI issues.

- Fat slows digestion
- Fiber can cause bloating and discomfort
- Excess protein doesn't provide quick energy and can feel heavy

Save these for post-ride recovery meals.

## Athlete Spotlight: Demi Vollering (Team SD Worx–Protime)

![Demi Vollering racing stage 8 of the 2024 Tour de France Femmes](/assets/images/journal/riders/demi-vollering.jpg)
*Photo: [JrPol](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Demi_Vollering_in_etappe_8_van_de_Tour_de_France_Femmes_2024.jpg), [CC BY-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/)*

Demi Vollering is having a breakout season—and it's not by accident. After a focused winter of polarized training and altitude blocks, she won at **La Flèche Wallonne**, cementing her status as the top climber in the women's WorldTour.

### Her Edge? Precision.

**High Carb Intake**  
She fuels with **80–100g of carbs per hour** during races—and does the same during training to ensure her body is adapted to the carb intake.

**Data-Driven Recovery**  
She adjusts training load based on **resting heart rate and sleep data**. When recovery markers are off, she scales back intensity.

**Polarized Training**  
Her winter was built on the polarized model: lots of easy endurance work, combined with very hard intervals. No junk miles in the middle.

**Altitude Blocks**  
Strategic altitude camps boosted her red blood cell count and VO2 max going into the spring classics.

### Savvy Takeaway

You don't need a WorldTour team car to apply these habits:
- **Try boosting your carb intake on long rides** Start with 60g/hour and work up to 80g+ if your gut can handle it.
- **Track sleep and resting heart rate to guide your recovery** If RHR is elevated 5+ bpm or sleep quality drops, consider an easier day.
- **Practice your race nutrition in training** Your gut needs to be trained just like your legs. Don't try new fueling strategies on race day.
- **Use polarized training** Make easy days truly easy (Zone 1-2) and hard days hard (Zone 4-5). Avoid the "moderate" trap.
