## This Week in Performance: Caffeine – More Than a Wake-Up Call

Caffeine is one of the most reliable performance aids in endurance sport. Beyond giving you a mental boost, research shows it can:

✅ **Reduce perceived exertion**, making hard efforts feel more manageable  
✅ **Improve endurance**, supporting sustained power output  
✅ **Sharpen focus and reaction**, especially in long or high-stress events

### The Science of Caffeine

**How it works:**
- Blocks adenosine receptors in the brain (reduces fatigue perception)
- Increases adrenaline release
- Enhances fat oxidation (spares glycogen)
- Improves neuromuscular coordination

### Guidelines for Cyclists

**Effective Dose:** 3–6 mg per kg of bodyweight (Grgic et al., 2019)

**Example dosing:**
- 70 kg (154 lb) rider: 210-420 mg
- That's roughly 2-4 cups of coffee or 2-4 caffeine gels

**Timing:** 45–60 minutes before your ride

**Low Dose Benefits:** Even <3 mg/kg can boost alertness without side effects (Spriet, 2014)

**Caution:** Too much can cause:
- Jitters and anxiety
- Stomach upset
- Sleep disruption
- Increased heart rate

**More isn't better.** Find your personal sweet spot.

### Practical Application

**For training rides:** 2-3 mg/kg (moderate dose)  
**For races or hard group rides:** 3-4 mg/kg (higher end)  
**For ultra-endurance events:** Consider split dosing (half before, half mid-ride)

**Pro tip:** Tolerance builds quickly. If you drink coffee daily, you may need slightly higher doses for performance benefits—or take a caffeine break 3-5 days before your event for maximum effect.

### References

- Grgic, J., et al. (2019). ISSN Position Stand: Caffeine and Performance. *JISSN*, 16(1), 1–15.
- Spriet, L. L. (2014). Exercise and sport performance with low doses of caffeine. *Sports Medicine*, 44(S2), 175–184.
- Ganio, M. S., et al. (2009). Effect of caffeine on sport-specific endurance performance: a systematic review. *J Strength Cond Res*, 23(1), 315–324.

## Athlete Spotlight: Jacky Durand

![Jacky Durand](/assets/images/journal/riders/jacky-durand.jpg)
*Photo: [Eric HOUDAS](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Jacky_DURAND.jpg), [CC BY-SA 3.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/3.0/)*

Few riders captured fans' hearts like **Jacky Durand**, the French master of the long-range attack.

### Career Highlights

- **Winner of the 1992 Tour of Flanders**
- **Multiple Tour de France stage victories**
- Known for attacking with **100+ km to go**

### Racing Style

**Fearless.** Durand often launched solo attacks with over 100 km remaining—moves that seemed suicidal but occasionally paid off spectacularly.

His breakaways didn't always succeed, but his **courage defined him**.

### The Durand Philosophy

*"I'd rather lose attacking than lose sitting in the pack."*

Durand understood something important: **playing it safe guarantees mediocrity.**

### Legacy

Progress in cycling isn't only about watts or results—**it's about taking risks, pushing limits, and being brave enough to fail.**

Durand won plenty, but he's remembered more for **how he raced** than what he won.

### Lessons from Jacky Durand

**1. Take calculated risks**  
Durand didn't attack randomly. He knew when the peloton was hesitating and seized those moments.

**2. Accept failure as part of the process**  
Most of his long attacks were caught. But the ones that succeeded became legendary.

**3. Race with courage, not fear**  
Fear of losing prevents you from winning. Durand raced to win, not to avoid losing.

**4. Entertain yourself**  
Durand loved cycling. His attacks weren't just tactics—they were expressions of joy and passion.
