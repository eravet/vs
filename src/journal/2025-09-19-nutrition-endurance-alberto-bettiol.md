## Science & Training Insight: Nutrition for Endurance

Here are some recent findings and best practices around fueling during long rides, especially relevant as we head into more hill rides:

### The Carbohydrate Gap

A 2022 study of elite male and female cyclists found that during pre-season, many are consuming **excess protein and fat**, while **under-consuming carbohydrates**.

**Key findings:**
- Carbs are especially important even in lighter training periods, not just race days, for recovery, maintaining training intensity, and health
- Micronutrient gaps also show up (vitamin D, iodine, etc.), so diet variety matters

**Sources:**
- "New Horizons in Carbohydrate Research & Application" by Podlogar et al. (2022)
- "Nutritional Habits of Professional Cyclists during Pre-Season" - Muros, J. J., Sánchez-Muñoz, C., Campos, D., et al. (2022)

### The 90g Per Hour Protocol

The same study explores updated ideas for how much carbohydrate to take, how it's used (oxidation), and how to do it more efficiently.

**Research shows:**
- **Mixing sugars** (e.g. glucose + fructose) allows higher carbohydrate intake and absorption without overwhelming the stomach
- There is increasing evidence that during hard efforts, intake rates of **90 g/hr may be more optimal** than traditional lower rates (60-75 g/hr), provided the athlete has trained the stomach and is doing sufficient work
- But more isn't always better: beyond a certain point, increases in carb ingestion show diminishing returns and may bring stomach distress

### Exogenous vs. Endogenous Fuel

The body uses **stored glycogen and liver glucose (endogenous)** plus what you ingest during exercise **(exogenous)**. 

Studies show that improving exogenous carb delivery can help sustain power and delay fatigue, especially in the later hours of long rides. But again, while exogenous carbs can be increased, they may not spare endogenous stores much after a certain threshold.

**Source:** "90 vs 120 g of Carbohydrates for Endurance Performance - Which is Best?" (Data-Driven Athlete blog)

## Practical Application: How to Fuel for Long Hill and Endurance Rides

**For 2–3 hour rides:**  
Aim for **60-80 g/hour** of mixed carbohydrates (drinks, gels, bars)

**For efforts of 3+ hours, or rides with repeated high power efforts:**  
Try to eat **90 g/hr**, if your stomach tolerates it

**Train your fueling plan:**  
Always practice your fueling plan in training, not just race/hill ride day—this builds gut tolerance

**Pre-ride fueling:**  
Prioritize carbs before rides. Meal 2-3 hours prior or snacks to top off glycogen

**Post-ride recovery:**  
Carbs + protein for recovery within 30-60 minutes

**Diet variety:**  
Keep meals varied; watch for micronutrients (vitamin D, iodine, etc.). Ensure you're not overdoing fat or protein at the expense of carbs

### Example 90g/Hour Fueling Strategy

**Hour 1:**
- 500ml sports drink (30g carbs)
- 1 gel (25g carbs)
- 1 energy chew (20g carbs)
- **Total: 75g**

**Hour 2:**
- 500ml sports drink (30g carbs)
- 1 bar (30g carbs)
- 1 gel (25g carbs)
- **Total: 85g**

**Hour 3+:**
- Continue alternating liquid + solid nutrition
- Listen to your gut—back off if experiencing distress

Bettiol practices **high carb fueling under racing stress**—a skill that translates directly to gran fondos and challenging group rides.
