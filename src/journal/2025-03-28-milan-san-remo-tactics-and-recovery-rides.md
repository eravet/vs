## Tactical Breakdown: Milan-San Remo 2025

The 2025 Milan-San Remo once again gave us an exciting show. The decisive moment came on the **Cipressa**, earlier than the usual Poggio. Pogačar, Van der Poel, and Ganna rode away from the others and were never in danger of getting caught.

Ganna had amazing resilience, getting dropped twice and making it back to finish 2nd—a testament to his mental toughness and physical strength.

### The Winning Move

If you have not yet watched Milan-San Remo, check out the last 20 miles on YouTube. **This race is a masterclass on race tactics.**

My analysis: **Van der Poel was not the strongest, but he was the fastest and won.** This distinction matters. In cycling, being the strongest doesn't always translate to victory. Understanding when to conserve energy, when to position yourself, and having the speed to finish is what separates good riders from champions.

**Key Takeaways:**
- The Cipressa can be just as decisive as the Poggio when the right riders attack
- Resilience matters—Ganna's ability to chase back twice shows mental fortitude
- Speed beats strength in the final meters
- Positioning and timing trump raw power

## Training Tip: Maximize Your Recovery Rides

Many cyclists overlook the **method** of true recovery rides. Here's how to do them right:

### The Recovery Ride Formula

**Keep intensity low**  
Your heart rate should be in Zone 1. If you're breathing hard, you're going too hard.

**Keep it short**  
45–60 minutes is enough. More isn't better on recovery days.

**Flat terrain preferred**  
Avoid hills that force you to push hard. Save climbing for quality sessions.

**High cadence (90+ rpm)**  
Spinning promotes better circulation and recovery without muscular strain.

### Why It Works

Recovery rides increase blood flow to fatigued muscles, flush out waste products, and enhance adaptation **without adding more fatigue**. 

**The bottom line:** If you're pushing too hard on recovery days, you're missing out on gains from your harder sessions. Recovery is when your body adapts and gets stronger—don't sabotage it by making easy days medium-hard days.

## The Recovery Mindset

Many cyclists struggle with recovery rides because they feel "too easy." Ego gets in the way. But the best cyclists understand that **easy days make hard days harder**. Embrace the recovery spin. Your legs will thank you on interval day.
