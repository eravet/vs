## Strong Training + Smart Recovery = Performance

Strong training is only part of the equation; **recovery is where gains happen**. This week we're looking at the science of sleep and spotlighting one of the most consistent riders in the peloton.

## Science: Sleep & Performance

Sleep isn't just rest—**it's one of the most powerful training tools you have**.

### The Research

Endurance athletes who extend sleep by **1–2 hours per night** improve reaction time, mood, and time-to-exhaustion (Fullagar et al., 2015).

**REM & Deep Sleep:**  
Growth hormone, critical for recovery and muscle repair, peaks during deep sleep (Van Cauter et al., 2000).

**Performance impact:**  
Even a single night of restricted sleep (<6h) can reduce cycling power output by **~5–10%** the following day (Skein et al., 2011).

### Practical Tip

Aim for **7–9 hours**, and when training load spikes, add a **nap (20–30 min)** to accelerate recovery.

### The Sleep-Performance Connection

**What happens during sleep:**
- Muscle protein synthesis peaks
- Growth hormone release
- Glycogen replenishment
- Neural pathway consolidation
- Immune system strengthening
- Inflammation reduction

**What happens with poor sleep:**
- Decreased testosterone
- Elevated cortisol (stress hormone)
- Impaired glucose metabolism
- Reduced glycogen storage
- Slower reaction times
- Poor decision-making

### References

- Fullagar, H. H. K., et al. (2015). Sleep and athletic performance: the effects of sleep loss on exercise performance, and physiological and cognitive responses. *Sports Medicine*
- Van Cauter, E., et al. (2000). Endocrine physiology of sleep. *Endocrine Reviews*
- Skein, M., et al. (2011). Sleep restriction reduces time-trial performance in elite cyclists. *Eur J Appl Physiol*

## Rider Spotlight: Primož Roglič

![Primož Roglič at the 2020 Slovenian time-trial championship](/assets/images/journal/riders/primoz-roglic.jpg)
*Photo: [Petar Milošević](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Primo%C5%BE_Rogli%C4%8D_(2020_Slovenian_Time_Trial_championship)_v3.jpg), [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/)*

From **ski jumper to Grand Tour winner**, Primož Roglič has built a career on resilience and relentless preparation.

### The Transformation

Roglič didn't start cycling seriously until his early twenties after a ski jumping career. Yet he became:
- **3x Vuelta a España champion**
- **Olympic gold medalist (time trial)**
- **Multiple Grand Tour stage winner**
- One of the most consistent GC contenders in the world

### The Roglič Recovery Protocol

**Consistency First:**  
Roglič is known for keeping his training steady year-round, avoiding extreme peaks and valleys.

**Sleep Discipline:**  
He's spoken about strict routines during stage races:
- Early dinners
- No screens before bed
- Prioritizing recovery over socializing
- Consistent sleep/wake times

**The mindset:**  
*"Recovery is non-negotiable. If I don't sleep well, I can't perform. It's that simple."*

### What Makes Roglič Special

**Late start, elite finish:**  
Proves it's never too late to develop world-class fitness

**Mental toughness:**  
Has overcome devastating crashes and near-misses to keep winning

**Systematic approach:**  
Every detail matters—nutrition, sleep, recovery, equipment

**Consistency over heroics:**  
Rarely has bad days because he prioritizes preparation and recovery

### Takeaway for Us

**Recovery is a skill, not an afterthought.**

Building simple, repeatable sleep habits can be the difference between just finishing and breaking through.

Small changes can pay off in watts:
- Cutting late caffeine (after 2pm)
- Using a wind-down routine (no screens 30 min before bed)
- Keeping bedroom cool (65-68°F)
- Blackout curtains or eye mask
- Consistent sleep schedule (even weekends)

## The Sleep Optimization Checklist

**Environment:**
- Cool temperature (65-68°F)
- Dark room (blackout curtains)
- Quiet (white noise if needed)
- Comfortable mattress/pillow

**Routine:**
- Consistent bedtime/wake time
- Wind-down ritual (30 min before bed)
- No screens 1 hour before bed
- Light reading or stretching

**Nutrition:**
- No caffeine after 2pm
- Avoid large meals 3 hours before bed
- Consider magnesium supplement
- Limit alcohol (disrupts REM sleep)

**Training timing:**
- Hard efforts in morning or afternoon
- Avoid intense training within 3 hours of bed
- Easy spin or yoga okay in evening

## The Bottom Line

You can't out-train poor sleep. The best training plan in the world won't work if you're not recovering.

**Sleep is performance.**

Treat it like intervals, nutrition, and bike fit—as a critical component of your training system.

**Sleep well. Ride strong.**
